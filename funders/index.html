Fund the people who make it work.

Support a practical 90-day cohort: existing digital skills learning, two Solve clinics, trusted neighborhood connectors, and paid time for residents.

A clear starting budget

Plan roughly $7,000–$16,000 for a cohort. This is a planning range, not an awarded grant or a promise of funded sessions. Confirm staffing, room costs, interpretation, and stipend administration before committing.

Participant time is budgeted at $30 per 90-minute session. Peer helpers and connectors are budgeted at $20 an hour.

What that makes possible

  • Neighbors practice checking AI answers and spotting scams.
  • Residents leave clinics with a sourced problem brief.
  • A named person owns the next step.
  • Peer helpers can help lead the next cohort.

Evidence, with its limits attached

The Linwood / LAMP snapshot includes 3,643 service requests opened in the trailing 365 days, using five official city neighborhood polygons. It is not the older 15-neighborhood bounding-box sample in the handoff research.

Service requests measure reported problems, not prevalence, resident identity, or program impact. Closure medians exclude open cases. Methods and provenance are included in the pack.
