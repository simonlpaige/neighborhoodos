Ready to build something?

Most neighborhood problems do not need new software. When one does, this is the path from a first lesson to a small tool, using free courses that already exist.

  1. About 15 minutes · NeighborhoodOS

    Learn to check an answer

    The free lesson: how chatbots work, how they get things wrong, and what never to type into one.

    Start the free lesson

  2. About 20 minutes · NeighborhoodOS

    Name one problem worth solving

    A one-page plan: the problem, who can fix it, the evidence, and a next step. Most problems end here, with a call, a form, or a meeting. That is a good result.

    Make a one-page plan

  3. Free online course · no coding

    Get comfortable with an AI assistant

    “AI Capabilities and Limitations” explains how these tools work. “Claude 101” covers everyday tasks.

    AI Capabilities and Limitations · Claude 101

  4. Free online course · no coding

    Learn to work with AI, not just ask it

    “AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations” teaches how to hand off work, describe what you want, judge the result, and stay responsible for it. There is also a version for nonprofits.

    AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations · For nonprofits

  5. Free online course · no coding

    Take a problem through to something that works

    “AI Fluency for Builders” follows one problem from idea to a finished solution. “Introduction to Claude Cowork” shows how an assistant can work on your real files and multi-step tasks.

    AI Fluency for Builders · Introduction to Claude Cowork

  6. In person · with a build helper

    Bring it to a build clinic

    Bring your one-page plan and what you learned. A build helper sits with you while you make a first version, checks it against the safety rules, and helps decide whether it is worth keeping.

    See classes and clinic updates

Before you build

  • Check that a tool is the right answer. A clearer form, a printed checklist, or a better referral may do more.
  • Keep private information out. No names, addresses, account numbers, or other people’s details.
  • Never build anything that scores, ranks, tracks, or decides for people. Read the safety rules.
  • A person reviews everything before it is sent, filed, or shared.

About the online courses

The courses in steps 3 to 5 are made by Anthropic, the company behind the Claude assistant. They are free and self-paced. You sign up with an email address on the course site, which is separate from NeighborhoodOS.

The thinking skills carry over to other AI tools. Some tools shown in the courses may need a paid account, so check before a group commits to one.

Listing a course is not an endorsement or a partnership. Course list checked November 15, 2026. See the full course catalog.

For organizers: you need one build helper, not a new curriculum

The courses cover the knowledge. They do not replace a person in the room. Plan for one build helper per cohort: someone who has finished the same courses, can sit beside a resident during a first build, and knows the safety rules. A volunteer from a partner organization can fill this role.
