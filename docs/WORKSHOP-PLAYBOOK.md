# NeighborhoodOS: The Workshop Playbook

> **"We thought we were building infrastructure. We were actually building curriculum."**

This playbook outlines the official curriculum and facilitation guide for running **The Workshop**: our resident-first, digital equity space designed in collaboration with **KC Digital Drive (KCDD)**. 

The entry point of NeighborhoodOS is always education and problem-solving, never the software platform. We run these workshops to teach residents what AI can and cannot do, bring real local issues into sharp focus, and co-design simple, community-benefit tools.

---

## 🧭 The Workshop Philosophy

Many AI workshops focus on administrative productivity or how to write marketing copy. Our workshop has a different moral center: **we design for the vulnerable resident**. 

We want to empower residents to move through four levels of advancement:
1. **Level 1 (Safe AI User)**: Understands how models work, how to spot scams and hallucinations, and how to protect their privacy.
2. **Level 2 (Local Problem Framer)**: Understands how to take a confusing neighborhood issue and break it down into structured stakeholder maps and municipal datasets.
3. **Level 3 (Tool Co-Designer)**: Learns how to wire public-record datasets and local models into simple guides or trackers.
4. **Level 4 (Peer Facilitator)**: Takes ownership of the workshop and teaches other residents.

---

## 📋 The 90-Day Pilot Schedule

Our standard pilot consists of three distinct phases: **Learn** (3 sessions), **Solve** (2 sessions), and **Build** (1-2 sessions).

```
[Month 1: LEARN] ───> [Month 2: SOLVE] ───> [Month 3: BUILD]
  AI Literacy           Problem Clinics        Simple Tooling
```

---

## 📅 Session Breakdown

### Session 1: What is AI (And What is it Not)?
- **Goal**: Demystify AI. Strip away the corporate marketing hype and explain large language models in kitchen-table language.
- **Key Analogies**: An LLM is like a superpowered auto-complete on your phone. It does not "think"—it predicts the next word based on patterns.
- **Activities**:
  - *The Paper Calculator*: Facilitator acts as a "hallucinating calculator" to show how models can confidently generate incorrect answers.
  - *Spot the Scam*: Residents review real phishing emails, voice clones, and deepfakes to learn how to defend themselves.
- **Output**: Personal privacy checklist for all participants.

### Session 2: Public Data and Private Rights
- **Goal**: Teach residents about local public data, open data portals, and individual data privacy rights.
- **Core Topics**: 311 municipal databases, property registers, and our **Civic Memory Safety Doctrine**.
- **Activities**:
  - *Municipal Treasure Hunt*: Finding housing code violations or dangerous building permits on their own block using public records.
  - *Surveillance Audit*: Reviewing a typical smart-city product and identifying why we ban resident tracking and scoring.
- **Output**: Map of local public-data assets for the participants' neighborhood.

### Session 3: Framing Local Problems (The "Solve" Clinic)
- **Goal**: Take a real, chronic neighborhood problem and break it down into a structured brief.
- **Activities**:
  - *The Sticky-Note Sieve*: Residents write local problems on sticky notes, then filter them by whether they are a "tech" problem, a "municipal communication" problem, or a "physical resources" problem.
  - *Stakeholder Mapping*: Identifying who is responsible (e.g. City codes, county assessor, state highways, or private landlords) and mapping public data points needed to hold them accountable.
- **Output**: 2-3 structured Problem Briefs chosen by the residents for the "Build" phase.

### Session 4 & 5: Co-Designing Community-Benefit Tools
- **Goal**: Take the highest-priority Problem Brief from Session 3 and co-design a simple, non-sensitive digital tool to help solve it.
- **Example Candidate**: A *Neighborhood Service Navigator* that takes a complex tenant issue, identifies relevant city codes, compiles links to resources, and provides an open progress-tracker.
- **Activities**:
  - *Paper Prototyping*: Drawing the interface on cardboard or paper cards first to test usability.
  - *Prompt Crafting*: Working together to write the system instructions for a local model, ensuring it has strict boundaries (e.g., "Always link back to the official city code. Never offer legal advice").
- **Output**: A fully specified tool brief with strict safety limits and human-review roles.

## 🛠 Facilitation Rules

1. **Speak like a neighbor**: Never use corporate tech slang ("AI-powered transformation," "stakeholder synergy," "scalable platforms"). Use clear, vivid, physical analogies.
2. **The software is not the hero**: The neighborhood residents are the heroes. The tool is simply a reason for neighbors to show up and coordinate reliably.
3. **No developer playgrounds**: Do not build a tool because it is technically cool. If it does not pass the "toothbrush test" (improving a resident's life daily or weekly), set it aside.
4. **Honesty over accuracy**: If our prototype fails or the city data portal goes down, tell the residents immediately. Never hide technical limitations or fake live data feeds.
