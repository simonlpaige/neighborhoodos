Guidance for planning a physical space where a neighborhood learns, solves problems, and runs its own AI tools. Written with a historic community campus in mind (such as the LAMP campus in Kansas City), but general enough for a library branch, community center, or church hall.

What a hub is for

  1. A room for Learn and Solve sessions. This is the core. Everything else is optional.
  2. Open hours where neighbors can get one-on-one help: checking a suspicious message, reading a city notice, drafting a letter.
  3. A home for local AI hardware, so tools run on site under community rules.
  4. A public wall (physical and online) showing problems raised, next steps, and what got resolved.

Space checklist

Learning room

  • Seats 25, movable tables (clinics need small groups)
  • Large screen or projector visible from every seat
  • A long blank wall for sticky-note clinics
  • Step-free access, accessible restroom, good lighting, acoustic control
  • Reliable Wi-Fi sized for 30 devices at once

Help desk / open hours corner

  • Two or three seats side by side (people help people, not across a counter)
  • A shared screen, a printer

Equipment closet or cabinet

  • Lockable, ventilated, not in a public room
  • Dedicated 20A circuit if it will hold a GPU workstation or server
  • Wired network drop, space for a UPS battery backup
  • Room to grow from one workstation to a small rack

Public wall

  • A visible board for the problem log and commitments

Planning construction (for spaces being renovated)

If a building is being renovated, it's much cheaper to plan for these now than to retrofit:

  • Extra electrical capacity and a dedicated circuit in the equipment room
  • Cooling or ventilation for the equipment room (a GPU workstation can put out as much heat as a space heater)
  • Conduit for network runs between the learning room, help desk, and equipment room
  • Enough outlets in the learning room for 25 people to charge phones and laptops
  • Blocking in walls for a large display and a sticky-note wall

Governance

A hub should have a short written operating agreement covering:

  • Who holds keys and admin passwords (at least two people)
  • Open hours and who staffs them
  • What the hardware may and may not be used for (Safety Doctrine)
  • How residents can see what data exists and request deletion
  • How decisions are made about new tools (use the Pilot Rubric)
  • What happens to equipment and data if the program ends (recommended: data deleted, hardware donated)

A sensible sequence

  1. Run Learn and Solve in any decent room. Prove demand.
  2. Add open hours with loaner devices and cloud tools used carefully.
  3. Add a single local workstation once a real tool needs it.
  4. Scale hardware only when usage justifies it.
