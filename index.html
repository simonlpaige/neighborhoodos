How the program works

Learn, solve, build, then teach.

Over about 90 days at the library: three Learn sessions, two Solve clinics, and one Build, only if a real need shows up. Participants are paid $30 a session. Each step is a rung. Nobody is graded, and anyone can stop at any rung.

  1. Three Learn sessions

    Safe AI user

    How chatbots work, why they make things up, the scams aimed at neighbors, and what never to type in.

    You leave withA privacy checklist, a family safe-word card, and the habit of checking the machine.

  2. Two Solve clinics

    Local problem framer

    Bring something stuck, or something Kansas City Documenters flagged from a public meeting. Sort out who can actually fix it, what public evidence exists, and the smallest next move.

    You leave withA one-page brief: who has the power to fix it, the public evidence, and a next step with a name on it.

  3. One Build, if earned

    Tool co-designer

    When a clinic surfaces a real need, sketch a small tool on paper, test it with neighbors, and write its safety rules.

    You leave withA tested paper prototype, or a small working tool with a named human reviewer.

  4. After the first 90 days

    Peer helper

    Co-lead the next cohort with the session plans and facilitator kit, paid $20 an hour. This is how the program outlives any one organization.

    You leave withThe materials and confidence to run the next session for your own neighbors.

The goal isn’t one more intro-to-AI class. It’s neighbors who can teach neighbors.

Our promise

Built to be bad at surveillance, on purpose.

Neighborhood data can help people or hurt them. These limits are written into the software itself and every program that uses the name agrees to them.

Our default: we hold nothing about residents.

Read the one-page threat model · Full safety doctrine

  • No files on residents. We never build profiles or histories of individual people.
  • No predictive policing. No hot-spot prediction, and no data sharing with police or immigration enforcement.
  • No watching organizers. No monitoring of speech, protest, or who influences whom.
  • No scoring people. No risk, trust, or “value” scores for residents or households.
  • No machine decisions about people. AI can summarize and draft. Humans decide who gets help.
  • No unreviewed AI meeting summaries. For public meetings we link to Kansas City Documenters’ fact-checked notes or the official record.

Run it where you live

Everything you need is in the open.

You don’t need permission, a license fee, or the original team. You need a library room, a facilitator, two neighbors who can bring twenty more, money for stipends, and someone who takes data seriously. No software required.

Questions people ask.

Do I need to know anything about technology?

No. The Learn sessions start from zero, and most of the program uses sticky notes, a projector, and conversation.

Is this anti-AI?

No. It’s pro-people. We teach where AI genuinely helps, where it fails, and how to stay in charge of it.

Does this replace 311 or city services?

No. It helps neighbors use them well, and keep track of what institutions promised.

What do you collect about me?

Nothing, by default. Sessions count heads, not names. If you want to hear about future sessions, the library keeps an optional list and deletes it after 90 days. Stipends are paid through the library or a fiscal partner. See the threat model.

Do I get paid to come?

Yes. $30 for each 90-minute session, the same $20-an-hour rate Kansas City Documenters pays residents to cover public meetings.

Do you summarize city meetings with AI?

No. We point you to Kansas City Documenters, whose notes are fact-checked by The Beacon, or the official minutes. Clinics are where you act on what you find.

How do I reach you?

Email simon@simonlpaige.com.

Can we do this outside Kansas City?

Yes. The education program works anywhere. The software needs new connectors for your city’s open data. The launch checklist walks through it.
