Learn 1

My privacy checklist

Check the ones you already do. Circle one to start this week.

Before I type into a chatbot

I leave out my Social Security number, bank or card numbers, and passwords.

I leave out medical details about me or my family.

I don’t paste in leases, contracts, or legal papers. If I need help, I remove names, addresses, and numbers first.

I don’t type other people’s private information.

When a chatbot tells me something

I check numbers, laws, deadlines, names, and links at the original source.

I use it for drafts and ideas. I make the decisions.

For legal, health, or money questions, I ask a real person too.

When a message or call feels urgent

My family has a safe word a stranger couldn’t guess.

If someone asks for money in a hurry, I hang up and call back on a number I already have.

I don’t tap links in messages I wasn’t expecting. I go to the official website myself.

Learn 2

Spot the scam

These are made-up examples of the kind of messages AI makes easy to write. Circle every warning sign you can find. Then compare with your group.

Text message · from an unknown number

Hi, this is Water Services. Our records show a past-due balance on your account at your address. Service will be shut off TODAY at 4pm. Pay now to avoid a $150 reconnection fee: bit.ly/pay-water-now

Email · “Neighborhood Assistance Program”

Congratulations! Because you live near 75th Street, you qualify for a $2,000 home repair grant from the city. To receive your funds, confirm your Social Security number and bank routing number by replying to this email within 24 hours.

Phone call · sounds like your grandson

“Grandma, it’s me. I’m in trouble, I got in a car accident and they’re holding me. Please don’t tell Mom. A lawyer is going to call you. I need you to get $800 in gift cards, right now.”

Warning signs to look for

  • A deadline measured in hours
  • A threat (shutoff, arrest, lost money)
  • A shortened or strange link
  • Asking for ID or bank numbers
  • Gift cards, wire transfers, or crypto
  • “Don’t tell anyone”
  • Local details that make it feel real
  • Money you didn’t apply for

What to do instead

Hang up or don’t reply. Look up the real phone number yourself (on your bill, or the official website) and call. Ask the family safe word. When in doubt, ask someone you trust before you pay anything.

Learn 3

Public data treasure hunt

Work in pairs. Pick a block you know. Find as many of these as you can in 20 minutes, using the city’s open data portal, the 311 map, and council agendas. Facilitator: write the local website addresses on the board.

FindWhat you found (and where)
An open 311 request near your block
How long a similar request took to close
A building permit issued nearby this year
A property on a dangerous or vacant buildings list
An upcoming council or committee agenda item that affects the area
One line in the city budget that pays for something on your block
Something you expected to find but couldn’t

The line we don’t cross

We look at places and institutions, not people. “Every unresolved streetlight request in our area” is fine. “Which neighbors complain the most” is never fine, even if the data could show it.

Learn 1 & 3

Quick check (anonymous)

Don’t write your name. Hand out at the start of Learn 1 and again at the end of Learn 3. Circle one: Before   /   After

1. A chatbot gives you the exact city code section your landlord is breaking. You…

Use it in a letter   Look it up on the city’s site first   Ask the chatbot if it’s sure

2. A call sounds like your grandchild asking for $800 right now. You…

Send it   Ask their birthday   Hang up and call back on a number you know

3. Is it safe to paste your whole lease into a free chatbot?

Yes   Only if I delete the chat   Not without removing private details first

4. Spot the error. A chatbot gave these answers. Circle what you’d do with each.

a. “You can report a missed trash pickup to Kansas City 311.”   Trust it   Check first   Don’t act on it

b. “Under Kansas City Code Section 56-9Q, your landlord must pay you back within 10 days.”   Trust it   Check first   Don’t act on it

c. “If your landlord ignores repairs, you can legally stop paying rent.”   Trust it   Check first   Don’t act on it

5. How confident do you feel using AI safely?

Not at all   1   2   3   4   5   Very

Facilitator key: 1 second answer, 2 third answer, 3 third answer. 4a trust or check (true, low stakes); 4b check first or don’t act (the section number is invented); 4c don’t act (legal advice a chatbot can’t give). Score the share of the five error items (1, 2, 3, 4b, 4c) answered safely, and note anyone rejecting 4a outright. Compare the group before and after; don’t match sheets to people.
